Overview

Supported by the National Science Foundation, this project builds a mobile app that makes walking easier for older adults. I designed and refined the app, worked with developers on feasibility, and ran the usability tests.

01Problem

More seniors use smartphones — maps weren’t designed for them

Mainstream map apps rarely match older adults’ changing abilities: research finds hundreds of issues caused by weak visual saliency, ambiguous affordances and low information scent [1].

02Research

What existing apps miss, and what seniors care about

Competitive analysisWeb survey

Two inputs shaped the design: a competitive analysis of current navigation apps, and a survey on what affects older adults’ walking experience the most.

Competitive analysis
Finding 01

Small text, complex screens

Hard for older adults to read and navigate.

Finding 02

Few multimodal aids

Voice and haptic guidance are rare in walking apps.

Finding 03

Sidewalk data is missing

Pedestrian paths are often absent or inaccurate.

Finding 04

No personal routes

Surface, safety and comfort are not considered.

Web survey

Surface condition, safety and comfort come first

These were the top three factors older adults weigh when choosing a walking route — so they became the inputs of the route recommendation and the overlays shown during navigation.

03Design

Five features built around senior users

Mobile accessibility guidelinesMultimodal guidance

Every feature answers a finding from the research. Click any screen to see it full size.

Feature 01

Text size comes first

The very first step is choosing a comfortable font size — readability before anything else.

Feature 02

Senior-focused points of interest

The five kinds of places older adults look for most — market, food, park, bench, restroom — are one tap away and can be filtered.

Feature 03

Clear map modes and highlighted sidewalks

Switch between 2D and 3D views; sidewalks are customised and highlighted with Mapbox. Voice, text and haptic guidance work together so a turn is never missed.

Feature 04

Comfort-aware routes

Our team’s routing algorithm weighs surface condition, traffic and crime safety, and thermal comfort. Stress and shade overlays show it on the map.

Feature 05

Weekly records & route survey

A record of the week’s walking, and a short survey after each route.

04User flow

From “where to?” to a finished walk

Search by voice or text, preview the route, navigate with overlays, then rate the walk. Ten screens, in order.

What worked

  • The simplified interface
  • Voice, text and haptic guidance together
  • The stress-level indicator for surface, traffic and thermal comfort

What I refined afterwards

  • Voice input and voice guidance accuracy
  • Missing back options and loading-status feedback
  • Making the stress / shade indicators easier to understand
06Outcome

Final interface

The app can help older adults walk more confidently and independently, with routes chosen for surface, safety and comfort.

Limitation

10 participants instead of ~30

A frozen recruitment budget cut the planned sample, which limits how far the findings generalise.

Next
